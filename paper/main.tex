\begin{abstract}
\textsc{FaithLM} scores an explanation by negating it and measuring how much the
target model's prediction shifts. We reproduce its COPA setting with the paper's
own models, obtaining $0.872$ against the $\sim\!0.85$ reported, and then subject
the metric to four controls it does not include. Three attack \emph{validity}: an
off-topic hint already flips the target on $55.4\%$ of instances; a hint written
for a different question flips it on $14.0\%$; and a target that picks the
first-listed option on $71\%$ of its parseable answers, at chance under
probability reading, is still scored $0.755$. One attacks \emph{reliability}:
redrawing the contrary hint a single time, with the explanation, question and
temperature unchanged, loses more than a third of all flips ($0.647$,
$[0.596, 0.694]$). Across six explainer\,$\times$\,target cells the score never
leaves $0.930$--$0.975$ and no comparison survives correction, so the noise
inside one measurement is an order of magnitude larger than the gaps the metric
is asked to resolve. We trace much of this to the released implementation
reading predictions by regular expression over generated text, which discards
$45$--$47\%$ of Vietnamese predictions. Reading choice probabilities instead, as
the paper's own formulation specifies, raises the flip rate from $0.374$ to
$0.509$ ($p=0.00011$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Explanations produced by large language models are fluent but need not describe
the computation that produced the answer
\citep{jacovi2020faithfully,turpin2023unfaithful}. \textsc{FaithLM}
\citep{chuang2026faithlm} proposes to settle this by intervention: an explainer
LLM writes a \emph{contrary hint} $\neg E_{\text{NL}}$ asserting the opposite of
the explanation, and fidelity is the resulting shift,
\begin{equation}
  S_E := f(X) - f(X \mid \neg E_{\text{NL}}).
  \label{eq:se}
\end{equation}
A large shift is read as evidence that the explanation carried decision-relevant
content.

We reproduce this measurement and then ask what a large shift actually licenses.
Our contributions:

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{Reproduction} of the COPA setting with the paper's own target and
  explainer (\S\ref{sec:repro}).
\item \textbf{Three validity controls} showing the score survives removing the
  explanation's content, and is high even for a target dominated by a position
  heuristic (\S\ref{sec:validity}).
\item \textbf{A reliability control} showing a third of all flips are properties
  of a single sampling draw rather than of the explanation
  (\S\ref{sec:reliability}).
\item \textbf{A concrete cause and fix}: the released code reads predictions from
  prose, not from the model's distribution (\S\ref{sec:parser}).
\item \textbf{A per-language XCOPA evaluation}. The original transfers COPA to
  XCOPA without naming a language; we report Vietnamese explicitly
  (\S\ref{sec:vi}).
\item \textbf{An audit of the trigger-prompt optimiser}, which in one of three
  runs never beat its human-written seed and in another scored the client's
  error string as a candidate prompt (\S\ref{sec:alg2}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

Faithfulness is commonly probed either by \emph{simulatability}, asking whether
a model can reproduce predictions from an explanation
\citep{madsen2024selfexp}, or by perturbing the input. \citet{jacovi2020faithfully} argue these test different
properties. Chain-of-thought text is demonstrably unfaithful under controlled
manipulation \citep{turpin2023unfaithful,lanham2023measuring}. \textsc{FaithLM}
avoids token deletion, which is ill-defined when the decision-relevant content is
latent, by intervening on meaning. Our concern is not that move but the absence
of a null condition, and the reliability of a score read from a single stochastic
draw.

\section{Dataset}

\paragraph{Balanced COPA.} \citet{roemmele2011copa} pose binary causal
commonsense questions; \citet{kavumba2019balanced} add mirrored instances to
suppress lexical shortcuts. We use the $500$-item test split.

\paragraph{XCOPA-vi.} \citet{ponti2020xcopa} translate COPA into $11$ languages
with professional translators. The Vietnamese test split contains \emph{the same
$500$ items} as the English split, verified item by item, so the pair is parallel
and language is the only variable. Neither corpus carries gold explanations,
which is why we cannot report the original's second metric
(\S\ref{sec:limits}).

\section{Method}

\paragraph{The measurement.} The target answers the question; an explainer
proposes $E_{\text{NL}}$ from the (question, answer) pair alone: it never sees
the target's own generation, which the pipeline discards after parsing, and its
instruction literally asks it to \emph{guess} the reason. The explainer then
writes $\neg E_{\text{NL}}$, and the target answers again with the hint
prepended.
An optimisation loop rewrites the explanation to maximise Eq.~\ref{eq:se} and
stops at the first non-zero score.

\paragraph{Validity controls.} \textbf{(A) Off-topic hint}: a hint unrelated to
the question (``The weather forecast mentions scattered clouds tomorrow
afternoon.''). \textbf{(B) Mismatched hint}: the contrary hint written for a
\emph{different} instance (same register, length and form, differing only in
which question it belongs to), so it cannot be dismissed as trivially ignorable.
\textbf{(C) Position bias}: we test whether a target's accuracy is explained by
always choosing the first-listed option, and if so, what fidelity that target
still earns.

\paragraph{Reliability control (D).} Keeping the winning explanation fixed, we
redraw the contrary hint once at the same temperature and re-ask the target,
scoring with the pipeline's own rule. If the score is a property of the
explanation, it should reproduce.

\paragraph{Score modes.} The released code computes
$|\Delta\text{accuracy}|$ over an argmax answer parsed out of generated text.
We add \textsc{prob\_accuracy} (argmax over choice probabilities) and
\textsc{logprob} (the continuous probability shift), both keeping
Eq.~\ref{eq:se} and changing only how $f$ is read.

\section{Experimental Setup}

For the reproduction we follow Table~2 of the original: $20$ steps, target
temperature $0.7$, explainer temperature $0.9$, top-$p$ $0.9$, $500$ instances,
target \texttt{phi-2} and explainer \texttt{gpt-3.5-turbo}
\citep{javaheripi2023phi2}. The original's explainers are no longer first-party
offerings: the reproduction reaches GPT-3.5-turbo through a third-party
gateway, and Claude-2 is not served at all. A separate axis therefore
substitutes four current explainers with everything else fixed, using the
paper's English prompts verbatim (diffed against the released commit; only
whitespace differs). Additional targets (Qwen3.5-4B and DeepSeek-v4-flash)
probe the Vietnamese setting. Where a Vietnamese prompt
scaffold is used it is a fluent rewrite rather than a literal translation of
the original strings, so the language axis varies wording as well as language. All runs, per-instance outputs, code, and a Colab notebook that rebuilds every
table below from the released results are public.

Three implementation details materially affect any reproduction.
\textbf{(i)} \texttt{do\_sample} is never set, so \texttt{transformers} ignores
the temperature the paper specifies and decodes greedily; $30$-instance probes
in both regimes agree qualitatively but are too small to certify equivalence,
and a run following Table~2 is not running the released configuration. \textbf{(ii)} The early-stopping condition is tested only
every fifth iteration, so $16.6\%$ of executed iterations occur after the
criterion is already met. \textbf{(iii)} 2026-generation models are
reasoning models: one target spent $179$ of a $200$-token budget on reasoning and
returned empty content, and one explainer spent $827$ reasoning tokens on a
five-token answer. Disabling reasoning eliminated empty completions across
$5{,}924$ calls and also brings these models closer to the paper's
non-reasoning originals.

\section{Results and Discussion}

\subsection{The reproduction succeeds}
\label{sec:repro}

With Phi-2 and GPT-3.5 on Balanced COPA, fidelity reaches $0.872$ over $500$
instances against the $\sim\!0.85$ the original reports. A second run of the
same configuration gives $0.860$, and a modern explainer (Gemini-3.5-flash,
$200$ instances, $8$ steps) lands on the same value. The measurement
reproduces.

\subsection{The metric is saturated}
\label{sec:ceiling}

In the second $500$-instance run the contrary hint flipped \emph{every}
parseable prediction: all $354$ correct and all $34$ incorrect answers score
exactly $1.0$ (the reproduction run differs by a single instance). Across a
full $3\times2$ explainer\,$\times$\,target grid ($N=200$ per cell) the score
never leaves $0.930$--$0.975$; pooled by target the two are indistinguishable
($0.943$ vs $0.950$, $z=-0.51$, $p=0.607$), and no explainer keeps its rank
across targets. Among four explainers from four laboratories, all six pairwise
$z$-tests are non-significant ($p=0.216$--$0.754$). One within-target pair
reaches a nominal $p=0.034$, but it fails the Bonferroni threshold, weakens to
$p=0.052$ under McNemar on the paired design, and reverses sign on the other
target. Doubling $N$ from $100$ to $200$ tightened the standard error from
${\sim}2.0\%$ to $1.5$--$1.8\%$ and still found nothing.

\subsection{Validity: the score is not specific to the explanation}
\label{sec:validity}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
 & \textbf{COPA-en} & \textbf{XCOPA-vi} \\
\midrule
Fidelity                & 0.860 & 0.336 \\
Control A (off-topic)   & 0.554 & 0.122 \\
\textbf{Corrected}      & \textbf{+0.306} & \textbf{+0.214} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Control A: $500$ instances each, Phi-2 target, GPT-3.5 explainer,
$20$ steps. Roughly $64\%$ of the English score survives when the hint has
nothing to do with the question.}
\label{tab:controlA}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:controlA} reports Control A. Control B, designed independently on
a different target and explainer, agrees: an own-question hint flips $0.370$ of
instances against $0.140$ for a hint belonging to another question
($p<0.0001$, $n=200$). Two controls differing in design, target and explainer
land in the same place: baselines of $0.122$ and $0.140$, corrected effects of
$+0.214$ and $+0.230$. The effect is real and semantic; roughly one flip in seven
is noise, and on English about two thirds of the headline number is
suggestibility.

Control C is sharper. On XCOPA-vi with English prompts, Phi-2 parses $117$ of
$200$ instances and picks the first-listed option on $83$ of them ($71\%$). A
bot that always picks first scores $62\%$ on this subset, leaving at most a
$9$-point margin over position alone, and under probability reading the same
model sits at chance (\S\ref{sec:vi}). A target this close to a position
heuristic is still scored at $0.755$ fidelity, with explanations that never
mention position and therefore cannot describe the preference that drives most
of its behaviour.

\subsection{Reliability: a third of flips do not reproduce}
\label{sec:reliability}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Arm} & \textbf{Flips} & \textbf{Repro.} & \textbf{95\% CI} \\
\midrule
Gemini-3.5-flash & 178 & 0.607 & [0.533, 0.676] \\
GPT-5.6-luna     & 187 & 0.684 & [0.615, 0.747] \\
\midrule
\textbf{Pooled}  & \textbf{365} & \textbf{0.647} & [0.596, 0.694] \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Control D. One redraw of the contrary hint, everything else fixed.}
\label{tab:repro}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:repro} is the result we consider most consequential. Holding the
explanation, question, target and temperature fixed and redrawing the contrary
hint \emph{once}, only $64.7\%$ of flips reproduce, far below a $0.90$ stability
threshold ($z=-16.1$) and significantly below $0.70$ ($p=0.026$). The two arms do
not differ ($p=0.12$), so this is a property of the measurement, not of an
explainer. A reported $0.930$ therefore means ``flipped on one draw'', not
``$93\%$ of explanations are faithful''; the two-draw-consistent rate is
${\approx}0.564$.

This also answers the obvious objection to \S\ref{sec:ceiling}, namely to run
more questions. The noise lives inside each single-instance measurement, ${\sim}35$
points of it, against inter-explainer gaps of ${\le}3.5$ points. Increasing $N$
narrows the error bar of a quantity that is itself unstable.

Flips found on the first iteration reproduce better than those found later
($0.715$ vs $0.609$ pooled, $p=0.041$), which is the signature one would expect
if the optimisation loop is partly harvesting lucky draws. We report this as
suggestive only: it is significant on one arm and not the other, and would not
survive correction for multiple comparisons.

\subsection{A cause, and a fix}
\label{sec:parser}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Score mode} & \textbf{Flip rate} & \textbf{95\% CI} \\
\midrule
\textsc{accuracy} (released) & 0.374 & [0.315, 0.432] \\
\textsc{prob\_accuracy}      & 0.460 & [0.400, 0.520] \\
\textsc{logprob}             & \textbf{0.509} & [0.449, 0.570] \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Qwen3.5-4B target, gpt-4o-mini explainer, $500$ instances attempted.
Flip rates and the McNemar tests against the released mode ($p=0.00011$ for
\textsc{logprob}, $p=0.01003$ for \textsc{prob\_accuracy}) are computed over
the $265$ instances whose baseline prediction was parseable; the
\textsc{prob\_accuracy}--\textsc{logprob} comparison uses all $500$.}
\label{tab:modes}
\end{table}

The released prompt ends at \texttt{\#\#\# Response:} without stating an output
format, then searches the continuation for \texttt{[choice]\ldots@}. On XCOPA-vi
this discards $45$--$47\%$ of predictions, at the same rate for two very
different targets: a prompt--parser mismatch, not a target failure. Among
surviving predictions the parser disagrees with a normalised argmax on $59\%$ of
$150$ audited instances. The metric also measures $\Delta$correctness rather than
$\Delta$prediction: on one instance the hint flipped the answer outright and the
released metric recorded $0.0$, because neither answer matched the gold string.

Table~\ref{tab:modes} shows what changes when $f$ is read from probabilities, as
\S3.1 of the original specifies. The gain is significant, but the informative
comparison is \textsc{prob\_accuracy} against \textsc{logprob}: $p=0.103$ at
$n=500$, and since $500$ is the entire test split this is a ceiling, not a
sample-size limit. \emph{Most of the improvement comes from abandoning the text
parser, not from the continuous signal.}

Table~\ref{tab:targets} shows an $80$-point accuracy range across targets, far
exceeding any effect the metric reports. Fidelity tracks the \emph{parse rate}
($41.5\% \to 6.5\% \to 2.0\%$ unparsed against $0.755 \to 0.945 \to 0.930$) more
closely than it tracks model capability, and the curve is not monotone in model
strength. Phi-2's Vietnamese accuracy is not evidence of anti-correlated
reasoning: Control C shows a strong position preference, and under probability
reading the same model sits at $0.520$ against a majority-class floor of
$0.530$, which is chance, with an $87\%$ preference for the first option.

A further variable is uncontrolled by the original's specification. With English
instructions over Vietnamese content, the paper's \texttt{exp\_instruction} never
names an output language, and explainers disagree: measured by diacritic
detection, explanation language ranges from $0.4\%$ to $75.3\%$ Vietnamese across
explainers while fidelity stays inside $0.930$--$0.975$. The metric cannot see a
difference that coarse.

\subsection{The trigger-prompt optimiser does not improve on its own seed}
\label{sec:alg2}

\midrule

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Algorithm~2 on XCOPA-vi: each run logs a seed, $14$ scored candidate
prompts and the returned prompt, scoring $15$ sampled instances per step.
\emph{Seed} is the human-crafted trigger prompt; \emph{Best} is the highest
score any optimised prompt reached afterwards.}
\label{tab:alg2}
\end{table}

The original's second algorithm optimises the \emph{trigger prompt} that elicits
explanations, reporting that optimised prompts outperform the human-crafted seed.
We ran it three times on XCOPA-vi (Table~\ref{tab:alg2}). In one run no
optimised candidate ever beat the seed. The trajectories oscillate without trend
and every run ends below its own best, so the returned prompt is not the best one
found.

Two properties of the setup explain why this is expected rather than surprising.
Each step scores on $15$ sampled instances, so a score can only take values
$k/15$ and the entire observed range spans a handful of instances. And because
the sample is redrawn each step, consecutive scores are not comparable: the
optimiser is being shown a noisy signal and asked to hill-climb on it, which is
the same reliability problem as \S\ref{sec:reliability} at the level of the
prompt rather than the instance.

One run also exposes an error-handling defect with direct consequences for the
result. The explainer client returns the literal string \texttt{"API error."} on
failure; the loop stores it as the next trigger prompt and scores it. Five of
sixteen records in that run carry \texttt{"API error."} as their prompt,
\emph{including the final returned optimum}. A failed call is not distinguishable
from a candidate prompt anywhere in the recorded trajectory.

\subsection{Conditioning the explainer on a self-explanation}
\label{sec:reasoning}

Because the explainer sees only the question and the parsed answer, its
explanation is a hypothesis about \emph{any} plausible reason for that answer:
two models emitting the same choice receive the same explanation. One variant
closes that gap by asking the target itself: a separate query
(``\emph{Please explain why you chose this answer}'', $T=0.7$, parsed from an
\texttt{[explain]\ldots@} span) elicits a self-explanation, which is appended to
both the explainer's prompt and the optimiser's trajectory prompt.

Fidelity drops from $0.850$ to $0.425$ ($n=200$, Qwen3.5-4B target,
DeepSeek-v4-flash explainer, Vietnamese prompt scaffold, $15$ steps) while mean
iterations rise from $6.09$ to $10.20$.

Two readings are available and our data cannot separate them. The variant may
be producing explanations that track the target more closely and are therefore
harder to negate \emph{dramatically}: under a metric rewarding large shifts,
being more faithful can score lower. Or the self-explanation may simply be
unfaithful in its own right, the very failure mode
\citet{turpin2023unfaithful} document, in which case the explainer is being
grounded in noise. Note also that this hybridises two settings the original
deliberately separates in its \S2.2: explanation by a third-party explainer, and
the target's own self-explanation. Deciding between the readings needs the
truthfulness metric, which our corpora cannot support.

\section{Error Analysis}

Parsing failures dominate the error mass everywhere: $112$ of $146$ errors in
the English control run are answers the parser could not resolve rather than
wrong choices, and in the $100$-instance pilots with a modern API target $13$
of $14$ errors across the English and Vietnamese runs are parsing failures.
What the pipeline calls accuracy is closer to a parse-success rate. The failure
cascades: when the answer is unresolvable the pipeline passes the literal
string \texttt{X} to the explainer, which explains it (``\emph{The model likely
chose answer X because it may not have a strong understanding of the
context\ldots}''), and the hint and score that follow are about an answer never
produced. This is why unparseable instances show a corrected effect of only
$+0.018$--$+0.044$: there is nothing to be faithful to, and the control detects
it.

Parsed answers hide three distinct failure modes. Phi-2's continuation after
the \texttt{Let's think step by step.} scaffold that scoring appends often
never states a choice in the format the parser expects. On Vietnamese input it
degenerates into word salad (gold ``\emph{Nó đã chết.}'', answer ``\emph{Nó
đáp án.}''). And targets asked in Vietnamese frequently answer correctly
\emph{in English} (``\emph{The kayak boat reaches the shore}'' for
``\emph{Thuyền kayak đến bờ}''), which exact string matching against a
Vietnamese gold scores as wrong.

\section{Limitations and Ethical Considerations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Single repetition.} The original averages three runs; ours are single
runs and we report no variance. This is the same gap we identify in the original
and we flag rather than repeat it silently.

\paragraph{Missing metric and baselines.} We do not report truthfulness, which
needs gold explanations that COPA and XCOPA lack; ECQA
\citep{aggarwal2021ecqa} would be required. The original's baselines
\citep{madsen2024selfexp,wang2022selfconsistency} are absent from the released
code and we did not reimplement them, so comparisons are against published values.

\paragraph{Scope of the controls.} Control D measures one redraw, on two of four
arms, and only on instances that already flipped; $64.7\%$ is therefore an
\emph{upper} bound on stability. The hypothesis that a weak target escapes the
ceiling is untested rather than refuted: our intended middle target turned out
to be strong ($90.5\%$).

\paragraph{Granularity.} Under the released mode $S_E$ carries one bit per
instance. A $30$-instance estimate of Vietnamese accuracy gave $33.3\%$ with an
interval containing chance, against $11.8\%$ at $n=500$; more than once a
$30$-instance probe suggested a conclusion the full run overturned.

\paragraph{Ethics.} All corpora are public. A first run of one explainer had
$23\%$ of its calls silently served by a fallback model under rate limiting; we
detected this through call accounting, quarantined the run, and fixed the client
to wait rather than substitute. We report empty completions from content
filtering rather than scoring them as wrong answers. No human subjects were
involved.

\section{Conclusion}

\textsc{FaithLM} reproduces, and its contrary-hint score is not empty: a real
semantic effect survives every control we ran. But the score as reported conflates
that effect with how readily the target follows any hint, rates a target
dominated by position preference at $0.755$, and loses a third of its flips to
a single resampling. Under language transfer the binding constraint is not the metric but
the regular-expression reader in front of it. We recommend that
intervention-based faithfulness measures report a null-hint baseline, sample the
contrary hint $k>1$ times and take a majority rather than stopping at the first
flip, and read the intervened quantity from the model's distribution rather than
from its prose.
